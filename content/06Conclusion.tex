events observed in experiments. Describing this radiation reliably presents
a number of theoretical challenges. This thesis has addressed two of them:
the clustering of QCD radiation into jets and the resummation of the large

In Chapter~\ref{chapter:DIS} we introduced a new family of inclusive
generalised-$k_t$ jet algorithms for Deep Inelastic Scattering, formulated in
the Breit frame and presented in Ref.~\cite{vanBeekveld:2026rez}. 
We described the clustering procedure and
introduced an ordering variable for the reconstructed jets --- the
light-cone fraction $z_i$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:zi} --- which provides an
infrared-safe prescription for identifying the final-state macrojet. We
also reviewed the existing jet algorithms used in DIS, outlining their
development and the ideas that led to our algorithm. To illustrate some
of the features of the algorithm, we performed a phenomenological study
comparing the angular-ordered, $k_t$ and anti-$k_t$ variants with the more
recently developed Centauro algorithm~\cite{Arratia:2020ssx}. The
comparison showed differences in the treatment of soft radiation and in
the amount of struck-quark radiation retained within the final-state
macrojet. We also investigated hadronisation effects and identified
observables that are particularly insensitive to hadronisation, providing
useful probes of non-perturbative physics in DIS.
The algorithms have been implemented as the \texttt{DISGenkt} plugin
to \textsc{FastJet}, distributed as part of \texttt{fjcontrib}.

Further work could investigate the non-perturbative effects identified in
this study, including through a re-analysis of existing HERA data. The
development of the Electron--Ion Collider (EIC) also provides an
opportunity to apply these algorithms to future DIS measurements. A
further extension of this work is the resummation of jet rates. These
observables contain large logarithms of the resolution parameter, which make
fixed-order predictions unreliable in precisely the region where the
clustering properties of the algorithm matter most. As discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:LTS-val}, this could be achieved within the \textsc{ARES}
framework by including the additional correction
$\delta\mathcal{F}_{\rm clust}$ and a single initial-state radiation leg.

In Chapter~\ref{ch:hhZjet}, we extended the \textsc{ARES}
framework to hadronic collisions. We established the ingredients required
for NNLL resummation in the presence of initial-state radiation and showed
that the \textsc{ARES} master formula retains the same overall structure
as in $\ee$ collisions, with the modifications associated with
initial-state radiation contained in the corresponding ingredients of
the formalism. In particular, we found that the inclusion of
initial-state radiation does not alter the structure of the cumulant. We
then derived the treatment of initial-state radiation in detail 
and applied it to one-jettiness. The resulting expressions were
compared analytically with existing results in the literature, providing
a validation of the initial-state radiation formalism.

The formalism established here provides the basis for NNLL resummation of
a broad class of observables in hadronic collisions. The next step towards
phenomenological predictions is the calculation of the process-dependent
hard coefficient for $hh\to Z+\mathrm{jet}$, which would complete the
ingredients required for matching the resummed result to fixed-order
calculations. Work towards this calculation is already underway.
Beyond $Z+\mathrm{jet}$ production, the same formalism can be applied to
other observables and processes. In particular, progress has already been
made towards its application to thrust minor and one minus transverse
thrust. The formalism could also be extended to higher logarithmic
accuracy and to processes with more hard coloured legs, although the latter
introduces additional complications through the colour structure of the
wide-angle corrections. 

The two areas of work in this thesis address different aspects of the same
underlying problem: understanding how QCD radiation is organised and how
its effects can be calculated reliably. The jet-algorithm study develops
new tools for identifying and studying jets in DIS, while the resummation
work extends the reach of NNLL predictions to hadronic collisions. Together,
these results contribute to the development of precision QCD phenomenology
for current and future collider experiments.
